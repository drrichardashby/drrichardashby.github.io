Literary and cultural scholar

Richard Ashby

I work on Shakespeare and its post-Holocaust afterlives across literature, theatre, film, television and philosophy.

Honorary Research Associate, Department of English, Royal Holloway, University of London.

Research

My research brings Shakespeare into conversation with Holocaust testimony, post-war theatre, continental philosophy, memory studies and questions of historical inheritance. I am particularly interested in what happens when canonical texts are read after catastrophe: how they are appropriated, resisted, transformed and made to bear witness to histories they could not have anticipated.

My published work has addressed King Lear after Auschwitz, Shakespeare and Primo Levi, Shylock and Jean Améry, the Roma genocide, Heiner Müller, Harley Granville-Barker and the state of exception, as well as questions of ethics, adaptation and political theology.

Shakespeare & Holocaust writing

Testimony, memory, catastrophe, adaptation and the ethical pressure exerted on literary inheritance after the Holocaust.

Post-war theatre & philosophy

Shakespeare in dialogue with twentieth-century drama and thinkers including Jean Améry, Primo Levi and Vladimir Jankélévitch.

Early modern afterlives

Race, religion, performance, political thought and the persistence of early modern forms in modern and contemporary culture.

Teaching

I have taught English literature, Shakespeare, theatre and adaptation at King’s College London and Royal Holloway, University of London, at undergraduate and postgraduate level. My teaching has included modules on Shakespeare and Holocaust writing, Shakespeare’s London, early modern comedy and identity, theatre and politics, premodern race and gender, literary adaptation, and film and performance.

I have also supervised undergraduate and postgraduate dissertations and contributed invited teaching at institutions including Yeshiva University, Edinburgh Napier University, the Shakespeare Institute at the University of Birmingham, and Semnan University.

Engagement

My public-facing work includes research collaboration, performance projects, interviews and writing for non-specialist audiences. Recent and ongoing collaborations have included Shylock Now with Deborah Leiser-Moore and a graphic adaptation project on Hamlet and the Les Vrais Riches diarist from the Łódź Ghetto with Tom de Freston.

I have written for History Today, contributed to the Digital Levi project, and discussed King Lear ‘After’ Auschwitz for the New Swan Shakespeare Center.
