Research

Current research

My work centres on Shakespeare and its afterlives in Holocaust testimony, post-war culture and philosophy, alongside new work on the literary and philosophical figure of the old mole.

Book in progress

Shakespeare and Holocaust Writing: Testimony, Literature, Philosophy

This book examines the presence and meanings of Shakespeare in Holocaust testimony and post-Holocaust thought. It asks what happens when Shakespeare is read, remembered, quoted and transformed in writing produced under the pressure of catastrophe.

The project brings Shakespeare into conversation with writers and thinkers including Primo Levi, Hélène Berr, Jean Améry, Hannah Arendt and Imre Kertész, while keeping the specificity of individual testimony and historical experience in view.

Book in progress

Without Remorse: Vladimir Jankélévitch, Macbeth, and the Holocaust

A book project bringing Vladimir Jankélévitch’s post-Holocaust writing on remorse and forgiveness into sustained dialogue with Macbeth.

The project considers what Shakespeare’s tragedy can and cannot do when read alongside a philosophy shaped by the problem of forgiveness after the Holocaust.

New project

The Old Mole

A new project tracing the literary and philosophical afterlives of the “old mole” from Hegel through nineteenth- and twentieth-century thought to Fred Moten and Stefano Harney.

I am interested in the mole as a figure for burrowing, underground labour, historical latency and unexpected emergence: a creature and metaphor through which writers and philosophers imagine forms of work, politics and historical movement that take place beneath the visible surface.

Earlier work

King Lear ‘After’ Auschwitz

My first book investigated Shakespearean appropriation in post-war British drama, with case studies on Edward Bond, David Rudkin, Howard Barker, Sarah Kane, Forced Entertainment and Dennis Kelly.
