Research

Current research

My current work centres on Shakespeare in Holocaust testimony, literature, and philosophy, alongside new work on the literary and philosophical figure of the old mole.

Forthcoming

Shakespeare and Holocaust Writing: Testimony, Literature, Philosophy

This book analyses the presence and meanings of Shakespeare in Holocaust testimony and post-Holocaust thought. It shows that Shakespeare was read, remembered, quoted and transformed in writing produced under the pressure of genocide.

The project brings Shakespeare into conversation with writers and thinkers including Primo Levi, Hélène Berr, Jean Améry, Hannah Arendt, Imre Kertész, and Emmanuel Levinas. It demonstrates that Shakespeare has been a constitutive presence in writing about the Holocaust, as writers grapple with the destruction of the humanist tradition in the camps and seek to develop new humanisms from the ashes.

Forthcoming

Without Remorse: Vladimir Jankélévitch, Macbeth, and the Holocaust

Can there be forgiveness without remorse? This book analyses Vladimir Jankélévitch and his uses of Macbeth in his post-Holocaust writing on remorse and forgiveness. It shows that Jankélévitch turns to Macbeth as an exemplary play of remorse — the condition that he felt was missing in post-Holocaust culture. Without such remorse, forgiveness for the Holocaust is impossible.

The book includes a chapter on the French playwright Enzo Cormann — a former student of Jankélévitch — and his Shakespeare-inspired play, Storm Still, and an epilogue that considers Jankélévitch and Macbeth alongside Derrida and his concept of the unforgivable.

New project

The Old Mole: Literary and Philosophical Burrowings

A new project tracing the literary and philosophical afterlives of the “old mole” from Hegel through nineteenth- and twentieth-century thought to Fred Moten and Stefano Harney.

I am interested in the mole as a figure of enlightenment, underground labour and revolution, the unconscious, negation, self-loathing and misanthropy, catastrophe, and Black opacity and fugitivity: a creature and metaphor through which writers and philosophers imagine forms of work, politics, sociality, race and history.

Earlier work

King Lear ‘After’ Auschwitz

My first book investigated Shakespearean appropriation in post-war British drama, with case studies on Edward Bond, David Rudkin, Howard Barker, Sarah Kane, Forced Entertainment and Dennis Kelly.
